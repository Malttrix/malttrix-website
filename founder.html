THE HUMAN REASON

I’ve always believed that creativity has the power to make people feel something meaningful.

A melody can bring comfort during a difficult night. A visual story can awaken a forgotten memory. A simple idea can change how someone sees the world.

Yet, somewhere between imagination and reality, many ideas never get the chance to grow. I know that feeling.

Malttrix began with a personal desire to change it — to create a place where ideas could be explored freely, shaped with care, and brought to life.

A place where imagination is not limited by convention, but supported by the structure it needs to become something real.

01 / THE BEGINNING

A curious mind. A love for art.

SOUND ↔ VISION

Ever since I was young, I’ve been drawn to new experiences, unfamiliar ideas, and the excitement of discovering something different. I was the kind of person who liked asking questions, exploring possibilities, and imagining what else might exist beyond what I already knew.

Among my many interests, art always held a special place. Music fascinated me because it could express emotions without needing an explanation. Visual art captured my attention because a single image could tell a story, create an atmosphere, or make someone pause and reflect.

I began to see art as something more than entertainment or decoration. To me, art is a way of connecting with people.

A song can make someone feel understood. An image can offer a moment of peace. A creative expression can give shape to feelings that are difficult to put into words.

I believe art can comfort, inspire, and gently touch people’s lives. That belief stayed with me as I grew older. And eventually, it became one of the reasons I wanted to create something of my own.

Some feelings are difficult to explain. Art gives them another way to be expressed.
02 / THE TURNING POINT

When imagination needed more room.

As I entered working life, I became familiar with the structures that shape professional environments. There were procedures to follow, responsibilities to fulfil, and expectations to meet.

Those experiences taught me the value of discipline, accountability, and organised thinking. But they also made me realise how easily creative ideas can become restricted by existing ways of doing things.

Sometimes, I had ideas I wanted to explore. Sometimes, I saw different possibilities or alternative ways to approach a challenge. Yet there wasn’t always room to try them. Not every idea fitted the established framework. Not every creative thought had the opportunity to become something real.

Over time, I began to ask myself a question:

What would it feel like to create without constantly having to fit my imagination into someone else’s boundaries?

I didn’t want to reject structure or responsibility. I wanted the freedom to think differently, explore unfamiliar ideas, and create things that genuinely mattered to me.

I wanted to bring together art, technology, storytelling, and the many interests that had shaped who I was. Most importantly, I didn’t want those ideas to remain possibilities that existed only in my mind. I wanted to give them a chance.

And I realised that if I couldn’t find the creative space I was looking for, perhaps I could build one myself.

I didn’t want to spend my life only imagining what could be. I wanted to discover what I could create.
03 / WHY I FOUNDED MALTTRIX

A place where imagination can take form.

IMAGINATION → FORM

Malttrix began with a simple belief: imagination deserves the freedom to grow.

I wanted to create an independent space where different ideas could be explored without being confined to a single discipline, format, or traditional way of thinking.

A melody could become a song. A visual idea could develop into an artistic experience. A question could grow into something that helps people think, learn, or see the world differently.

I wanted the freedom to create what I genuinely believed in. But I also wanted those creations to reach beyond my own imagination. To be seen. To be heard. To be experienced. And perhaps, to become meaningful to someone else.

That’s why I founded Malttrix｜梦萃. Not simply to establish a business, but to build a home for independent creativity — where original ideas can develop their own identities while being supported by a shared foundation.

A place where imagination is encouraged, creativity is respected, and thoughtful systems help good ideas move forward.

Malttrix is not a finished destination. It is a growing creative journey, built through exploration, experimentation, and the belief that even the smallest idea deserves a chance to become something meaningful.

Born from imagination. Refined into form.
04 / MY APPROACH

A creative heart. A structured mind.

I’ve always been interested in two sides of creation. The first is emotional and imaginative. It is the part of me that loves music, visual expression, storytelling, and the freedom to explore ideas without knowing exactly where they might lead.

The second is analytical and structured. I enjoy understanding how things work, solving problems, connecting different elements, and building systems that can support long-term growth.

For a long time, these interests seemed unrelated. Eventually, I realised that they could complement each other. Creativity gives an idea its character and meaning. Structure provides the foundation that allows it to grow.

This is also what my role as Creative Systems Architect means to me. I work on connecting creative direction, brand identity, digital technology, and the operational processes that help ideas become real experiences.

Sometimes that means shaping a brand’s identity. Sometimes it means developing a creative concept into something people can see, hear, or use. Other times, it means exploring how AI and digital systems can support research, content production, and everyday operations.

I don’t believe technology should replace the human feeling behind creativity. I believe it should give me more room to create with freedom, consistency, and care.

And I don’t believe every creative idea needs a complicated system. It needs the right one.

07 / WHAT I’M STILL LEARNING

Freedom also means responsibility.

Building Malttrix is teaching me that creative freedom means taking responsibility for what I choose to create.

01

Begin before it is perfect.

Even a carefully considered vision needs real action. Sometimes I need to stop refining and give an idea the chance to meet the world.

02

Make room for another perspective.

Independent thinking becomes stronger when I listen, question my assumptions, and remain willing to change a plan.

03

Let growth take its time.

I am learning to balance ambition with patience. Malttrix is not proof that I have every answer; it is part of how I continue to learn.
